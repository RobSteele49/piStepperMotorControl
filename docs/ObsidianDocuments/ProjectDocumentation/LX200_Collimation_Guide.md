# Collimation Guide: Meade LX200 10" Classic SCT
*Optimized for CGEM/GEM Mount Users*

## Overview
Collimation is the process of aligning the optical axes of the telescope's mirrors. For an SCT, this is achieved by adjusting the three screws located on the secondary mirror housing at the front of the tube.

## Prerequisites
* **Thermal Equilibrium:** Let the scope sit outside for at least 60–90 minutes. Heat plumes inside the tube will mimic bad collimation.
* **Good "Seeing":** If stars are twinkling violently, the atmosphere is too turbulent for fine collimation.
* **The Right Tool:** Usually a Phillips head screwdriver or a hex key (depending on whether you have original screws or aftermarket "Bob's Knobs").
* **High Magnification:** Use a high-power eyepiece (e.g., 9mm to 12mm) or a crosshair reticle eyepiece.

---

## The Step-by-Step Process

### 1. Rough Alignment (The "Donut")
1. Point the telescope at a bright star (Polaris is ideal as it doesn't move) and center it perfectly.
2. Defocus the star until it looks like a large "donut" with a black hole in the center.
3. Observe the "hole" (the shadow of the secondary mirror). 
    * If the hole is perfectly centered in the donut, your rough collimation is good.
    * If the hole is closer to one side, you need to adjust.



### 2. The Adjustment Logic
> **Rule of Thumb:** You want to move the "shadow" toward the side that has the most "meat" (the widest part of the donut).

1. Identify which way the star needs to move in the field of view to center the shadow.
2. Turn **one** of the three collimation screws a tiny amount (1/16th of a turn).
3. **Crucial:** The star will move out of the center of your eyepiece. Use your CGEM hand controller to **re-center the star**. 
4. Check the donut again. If the shadow moved closer to the center, you chose the right screw/direction. If it got worse, go the other way.

### 3. Fine Alignment (The Airy Disk)
Once the "donut" looks symmetrical, it's time for high-resolution adjustment.
1. Bring the star closer to focus until the donut becomes a small, bright point surrounded by one or two faint rings (the Airy Disk).
2. If the rings are not perfectly concentric (e.g., they look like a "comet" or are squashed to one side), repeat the adjustment process using microscopic turns.
3. Always finish with a **Counter-Clockwise** turn of the focus knob to seat the mirror against gravity.

---

## Pro-Tips for the LX200
* **Safety First:** If you are pointing the scope upward, be extremely careful not to drop your screwdriver/wrench onto the corrector plate. Some users tether their tools to their wrist.
* **Don't Over-tighten:** If a screw feels very tight, do not force it. Instead, loosen the other two screws slightly to "give" the mirror more room to tilt.
* **The "Final Touch":** Collimation should be the last thing you do before an imaging session. On a GEM mount like the CGEM, a meridian flip can occasionally cause a tiny shift in alignment; check the Airy disk after the flip for maximum precision.
